THE CHANGING FACE OF CASH

In March 2016, ₹500 and ₹1,000 together made up most of the value of India’s banknotes. One year later, the currency mix looked completely different.

RBI’s end-March denomination data shows the transition: ₹1,000 nearly disappeared, ₹2,000 became the largest denomination by value, and ₹500 later regained the top position.

THE REPORTING QUESTION

What explains the lasting shift in India’s denomination mix — and what does the data alone leave unanswered?

What the data shows

₹500 represented about 47.8% of banknote value in March 2016, fell to about 22.5% in March 2017, and reached about 51.0% in March 2019.

What the data does not prove

The denomination data shows composition and change. By itself, it does not establish why people, businesses or banks held particular denominations.

ABOUT THE DATA

Methodology and limitations

The annual chart uses end-March denomination-wise banknote data for 2016–2019. The workbook also contains selected RBI historical observations from September–November 2024 and August 2026; these are not a continuous weekly archive.

₹1,000 and ₹2,000 are kept in historical context rather than presented as ordinary current circulating denominations.
